## Lab mission statement (paraphrase — safe to adapt)

The lab studies how cell-intrinsic and environmental metabolic changes shape
immune cell function, with the long-term goal of finding immunometabolic
mechanisms that can be targeted to improve cancer immunotherapy. Nutrient
restriction, hypoxia, and the buildup of metabolic waste in the tumor
microenvironment all blunt antitumor immunity — but the mechanisms are largely
unknown. The lab uses **metabolomics, CRISPR screens, and rigorous mouse models**
to find targetable pathways.

## Usable quotes from Sam (Burroughs announcement, 2023)

> "I'm really interested in understanding how metabolism — things like diet,
> obesity or changes in the cellular microenvironment — change the way that
> immune cells work."

> "By understanding how that happens, we can potentially develop new therapies or
> vaccine approaches that let us choose the immune response that we want by
> changing the intrinsic metabolism of immune cells."

## Four research themes (map onto the Research-page project cards)

Each is grounded in the lab's real publications and current member projects.

1. **Mitochondrial metabolism in T cell function** — the core. Signature
   findings: mitochondrial complex III is essential for regulatory T (Treg) cell
   suppressive function (2019 *Nature*, Sam first author), and mitochondrial
   respiration is required for CD8⁺ T cell proliferation and fate (2025 *Nature
   Immunology*).
2. **Dendritic cell metabolism & anti-tumor / antiviral immunity** — the
   Burroughs-funded direction. Ties to Yichen's CRISPR-screen work on dendritic
   cells in the tumor microenvironment and Nour's work on DC Complex I
   dysfunction in antiviral immunity.
3. **The metabolic microenvironment & immune adaptation** — lactate/acidity,
   succinate, and AMPK-driven Treg adaptation to harsh microenvironments (2025
   *JCI*, Sam co-senior).
4. **Immunometabolism in human disease** — metformin and viral infection (Nour's
   second project), mitochondrial disorders, and inflammation; the translational,
   clinically-facing arm.
